## PATH A: Become a Vision TPA (Replace Envolve)

### What Envolve Actually Does

Envolve Vision (now doing business as **Centene Vision Services**, subsidiary of **Centene Corporation** — $178B revenue in 2025) acts as the middleman between health plans and vision providers in PR:

```
Health Plan (MMM, MCS, etc.)
    ↓ contracts Envolve to manage vision benefits
Envolve Vision (TPA)
    ↓ contracts with providers, sets rates, adjudicates claims
Your Mom's Clinic (provider)
    ↓ submits vision claims to Envolve
Envolve pays provider at negotiated rate (~65% of billed)
```

**Envolve's PR presence (confirmed from their Q1 2025 fact sheet):**
- Serves: **MMM**, **MCS Classicare**
- Part of Centene's network of 26,000+ eye care providers nationally
- 18 million members across 32 states + PR
- Uses Availity as electronic clearinghouse (Payer ID: 56190)

### The PR Vision Market

- **~835 registered optometrists** in Puerto Rico (NPI database)
- Plus ophthalmologists (estimated ~200-300)
- **~3.2 million** PR population
- Major MCOs with vision benefits: MMM, MCS, Triple-S, First Medical, Humana PR (exiting 2026)
- Most vision benefits routed through Envolve or similar TPAs

### Why Providers Hate Envolve

1. **~35% admin fee** — providers keep ~65 cents on every dollar
2. **Slow payments** — 30-90 day payment cycles
3. **Opaque adjudication** — hard to dispute denials
4. **Mainland corporate** — no local presence, language barriers
5. **Take-it-or-leave-it contracts** — can't negotiate rates as individual provider
6. **Poor technology** — their portal (Eye Health Manager) is outdated

### Your Competitive Advantages

1. **Local + bilingual** — you're PR-based, understand the market
2. **Modern tech** — SometeoPR is already built (claims processing, adjudication, denials, ERA reconciliation)
3. **Provider relationships** — your mom knows other optometrists who hate Envolve
4. **Lower overhead** — no massive corporate structure to feed
5. **Provider-first mentality** — you come from the provider side, not the payer side
6. **Timing** — PR TPA licensing just became regulated (Rule 111, March 2025), creating a legitimate barrier that actually helps you once you're in

### Step-by-Step Roadmap

#### Phase 0: Preparation (Month 1-2) — $5-10K

| Task | Details | Cost |
|------|---------|------|
| **Form PR LLC/Corp** | Register business entity with PR State Department | ~$500 |
| **Get TPA License** | Apply with PR OCI under Rule 111 (see below) | $2,500/year |
| **E&O Insurance** | Errors & Omissions — required by MCOs before contracting | $5-15K/year |
| **HIPAA Compliance** | Policies, procedures, BAAs, security assessment | $2-5K |
| **Hire actuary (consultant)** | For rate-setting and risk analysis — MCOs will ask for this | $2-5K one-time |
| **Legal review** | Provider contracts, MCO contract templates | $3-5K |

**PR TPA License (Rule 111) Requirements:**
- Submit Uniform Application to OCI (Oficina del Comisionado de Seguros)
- Annual fee: **$2,500**
- Fidelity bond required
- Designate compliance officer
- Notify OCI within 30 days of any material changes
- Keep books & records for minimum 5 years
- File: https://docs.pr.gov/files/OCS/English/Legal%20Affairs/Regulations/Insurance%20Code%20Regulations/Rule%20111-TPA%20License_english.pdf

#### Phase 1: Build Provider Network (Month 2-4) — $0

This is free and it's your biggest weapon.

1. **Start with your mom** — she's your pilot customer
2. **Talk to her colleagues** — every optometrist she knows
3. **Pitch:** "What if I could get you the same patients but you keep 80-85% instead of 65%?"
4. **Target:** Get 15-25 providers to sign letters of intent
5. **Create provider agreement template** — simpler and more transparent than Envolve's
6. **Build a provider directory** (SometeoPR already has provider management)

**What providers need to hear:**
- "Same patients, better reimbursement"
- "Local company, you can actually talk to someone"
- "Modern system — real-time eligibility, instant claim status, mobile-friendly"
- "Transparent fee structure — you see exactly what we take and why"

#### Phase 2: Approach One MCO (Month 4-8) — $0-5K (travel/meetings)

You don't need all MCOs. Start with ONE.

**Best first target: MMM Multi Health**
- Why: Already uses Envolve for vision, large member base in PR
- Centene owns both MMM and Envolve... BUT MCOs regularly evaluate TPA alternatives
- If not MMM, try **MCS** or **Triple-S** — they may not use Envolve at all, or may want a local option

**The pitch to the MCO:**
1. "We're a PR-based vision benefit administrator"
2. "We have a network of [X] optometrists who want to work with us"
3. "We can administer your vision benefit for less than Envolve charges you"
4. "Our providers are happier, which means better HEDIS scores for you" (MCOs CARE about this)
5. "We have modern technology — faster claims processing, real-time eligibility, bilingual support"

**What the MCO will ask you:**
- Provider network adequacy (do you have enough providers to cover the island?)
- Claims processing capability (SometeoPR handles this)
- Accreditation / compliance (HIPAA, URAC if they require it)
- Financial stability (do you have reserves to pay claims?)
- Rate proposals (what will you charge vs Envolve?)

#### Phase 3: Go Live (Month 8-12)

1. Sign MCO contract
2. Credential providers in your network
3. Begin accepting and adjudicating vision claims through SometeoPR
4. Post payments to providers (you need capital for this — see below)

**Capital requirement:** You'll need working capital to pay providers before MCO pays you. Typical MCO payment cycle is 30-45 days. If you process $200K/month in claims, you need ~$200-400K in reserves. Options:
- Personal savings / investor
- Small business loan (SBA)
- Factor the receivables (expensive, last resort)
- Negotiate weekly or bi-weekly MCO payments (some do this for small TPAs)

#### Phase 4: Scale (Month 12-24)

1. Add more MCOs
2. Grow provider network
3. Add ophthalmologists (higher-value procedures)
4. Expand to dental? (same TPA model)
5. Potentially expand to other Caribbean islands

## PATH B: Become a Clearinghouse (Replace Inmediata)

### What Inmediata Actually Does

```
Provider / Billing Software
    ↓ sends 837P claim file
Inmediata (clearinghouse)
    ↓ validates, scrubs, routes to correct payer
Payer (Triple-S, MCS, MMM, etc.)
    ↓ adjudicates, sends 835 ERA back
Inmediata
    ↓ delivers ERA to provider
Provider reconciles payment
```

### The Clearinghouse Moat

The hard part isn't the software — it's the **payer connections.** Each connection requires:

1. **Trading Partner Agreement** — legal contract with each payer
2. **Connectivity testing** — 3-6 months per payer, validating every transaction type
3. **Companion guide compliance** — each payer has unique business rules
4. **Ongoing maintenance** — payers change rules, update systems, require recertification
5. **24/7 monitoring** — payers expect clearinghouses to be always-on

Inmediata has **20+ years** of these relationships in PR. Building from scratch is expensive.

### Two Approaches

#### B1: Full Clearinghouse (Hard Mode) — $100-200K, 12-24 months

Build your own payer connections:

| Requirement | Cost | Timeline |
|-------------|------|----------|
| EDI translator / validator | Already built (SometeoPR) | — |
| Payer connections (10+ PR payers) | $10-50K per payer | 3-6 months each |
| SOC 2 Type II certification | $20-50K/year | 3-6 months initial |
| HIPAA compliance infrastructure | $5-10K | 1-2 months |
| E&O insurance | $5-15K/year | — |
| Operations monitoring | $3-5K/month | — |
| Staff (EDI analyst, support) | $60-100K/year | — |

**Revenue:** 500 providers × $100/month = **$50K/month** ($600K/year)
Or per-claim: 500K claims/year × $0.25 = **$125K/year**

**Verdict:** Expensive, slow, moderate revenue. Not worth it as a standalone business for PR market.

#### B2: Clearinghouse Reseller (Smart Mode) — $10-20K, 3-6 months

Use **Stedi** (or another programmable clearinghouse) as your backend:

| Requirement | Cost | Timeline |
|-------------|------|----------|
| Stedi Developer plan | $500/month | Immediate |
| Your UI (SometeoPR) | Already built | — |
| Provider onboarding | $0 (your time) | — |
| HIPAA compliance | $5-10K | 1-2 months |

**How it works:**
1. Provider uses SometeoPR (your UI)
2. SometeoPR generates 837P → sends to Stedi via API
3. Stedi routes to payer
4. Payer sends 835 → Stedi delivers to SometeoPR
5. You charge provider $50-75/month (less than Inmediata's $140)

**Revenue:** 100 providers × $75/month = **$7,500/month** - $500 Stedi = **$7,000/month**
At 500 providers: **$37K/month** ($444K/year)

**Verdict:** Much more achievable. You're essentially selling SometeoPR as a SaaS with built-in clearinghouse. The value prop vs Inmediata: cheaper, modern UI, integrated with Wink, bilingual.

**⚠️ BUT:** Stedi may not support all PR-specific payers (Menonita, some Reforma plans). You'd still need Inmediata as backup for those. So this becomes "cheaper for most claims, fallback to Inmediata for edge cases."

The real move is combining Path A and Path B2:

### Year 1: Vision TPA + SaaS Billing
